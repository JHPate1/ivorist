How Flow works, as a hand would.

Traditional tools break a thought into dialogs: a keyframe here, a tween there, an easing panel somewhere else. Flow keeps the thought whole.

01
Stroke the motion

Draw the path your subject should travel. The curve is not a guide for later math, it is recorded as the motion itself.

02
Your hand sets the timing

How fast you draw the stroke becomes the pacing. Slow down for weight, speed up for snap, no funnel of dialogs in between.

03
Objects ride the stroke

Existing objects can be grabbed and moved, and Strova turns that move into animation the same way, so choreography works with things as well as sketches.

Three commitments, nothing rented.

FlowMOTION AS DRAWN

Animation by gesture, for strokes and objects alike. The timing of your hand is the law of the scene; editing tools exist to refine that law, never to replace it.

Custom brushesBUILT FOR YOUR NEEDS

Brushes are yours to make: shape, texture, feel, behavior. Tune one for your project, keep it portable, share it if you like. A community's brush kit is an inheritance, not a subscription.

Rapid exportingSHORT LOOP TO FILE

Getting work out must never be slower than making it. Exports run against the same render the preview uses, so the file you share looks exactly like the motion you made.

What "20x smoother" is made of.

The playback target is roughly twenty times less latency and judder than today's tools on the same hardware. It lives in engine architecture:

The playback path never touches the disk mid-render. Frames are composed in memory and stay there until the shot ends.

One rasterization is shared by preview and export, so the smoothness you see is the smoothness you ship.

The scene is one living graph, not a stack of frames being shuffled, so scrubbing and playback read the same data.
