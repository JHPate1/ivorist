PROJECT 003 / SOFTWARE

Strova.

IN DEVELOPMENT

An open source 2D animation engine that keeps up with you. Draw a stroke and it moves. Move an object and it comes alive. Targeting a release in Q1 2027.

THE IDEA

Animation that moves when you do.

Traditional animation tools make you split a thought into five dialogs: a keyframe here, a tween there, an easing curve somewhere else. The result is software that technically works, but feels like filling in paperwork about the motion rather than making it.

Strova starts from the gesture. You draw a stroke, or move an object, and the motion you performed becomes the animation. Timing is captured from how you did it, not from a panel you configure afterward. The system is built around that one idea, and everything else serves it.

Strova is open source. Real artists deserve tools whose bones they can read, and a community should never lose its work to a company that stops caring. The source stays open, and the project releases in Q1 of 2027.

THE INSTRUMENTS

Built around motion.

Three pillars carry the engine design.

01

Flow

Make an animation by simply stroking or moving objects. The path you draw is the motion, the pace of your hand is the timing. Choreography at the speed of sketching.

02

Custom brushes

Brushes are not presets you rent. Build your own for your own needs, tune their feel, texture, and behavior, and keep them as yours, shareable and portable.

03

Rapid exporting

Getting your work out should never be slower than making it. Rapid exporting pushes finished frames to video in seconds, keeping the loop from idea to shareable file short.

WHAT WE MEAN BY SMOOTH

20x smoother, on the same hardware.

Strova is engineered to be dramatically smoother than the tools animators work in today, and the number is taken seriously: a playback target of roughly twenty times less latency and judder on the same machine, achieved in the engine rather than with fancy export settings.

That comes from architecture: a playback path that never touches disk mid-render, cached rasterization shared between preview and export, and a timeline model that treats a scene as one living graph instead of frames to shuffle. The smoothness is a property of the engine, not a mode you switch on.

Strova is in active development and has not shipped yet. The current estimate for a first public release is Q1 of 2027. Like any good story, the date may shift, but the promise, open source, does not.
