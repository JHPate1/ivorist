THE IDEA

Research should start with what you already have.

Important information is often spread across documents, notes, reports, and other files. Finding it can be difficult, especially when you remember the subject but not the exact wording or location.

Archivist is designed to work with documents you provide or files you choose to make available to it. Rather than relying only on a web search or general model knowledge, it uses the contents of those files as the material for research.

The goal is to make a collection of documents easier to search, understand, and reason about as a whole.

HOW IT WORKS

From documents to useful answers.

The intended workflow is built around the information in your files, not just the language model's ability to generate text.

01 / INPUT

Provide your files

Select documents or provide files for Archivist to process. Their text is extracted and prepared for searching.

02 / RETRIEVAL

Find relevant material

When you ask a question, Archivist searches the indexed content for passages that are semantically related to what you asked.

03 / REASONING

Build an answer from evidence

Retrieved passages provide context for the language model. The aim is to produce answers and inferences that are supported by the documents.

GROUNDING AND ACCURACY

Answers should be based on evidence.

Finding relevant text is only part of the process. Archivist is intended to make the reasoning behind an answer more reliable and easier to inspect.

01 / SOURCE MATERIAL

Use the files as context

Answers should be informed by the documents the user has provided, rather than treating general model knowledge as a substitute for those sources.

02 / REASONING

Make supported inferences

Archivist should be able to connect information across multiple passages and draw reasonable conclusions when the available evidence supports them. Those conclusions should not be presented as direct quotations or established facts.

03 / TRANSPARENCY

Keep answers traceable

A key development goal is to make it easier to identify which files and passages support an answer, so users can check the underlying information themselves.

04 / LIMITATIONS

Recognize missing information

If the provided material does not contain enough evidence to answer a question, Archivist should communicate that limitation instead of filling the gaps with speculation.

CURRENT DEVELOPMENT

Building the foundation.

The current work is focused on making the core document retrieval process function reliably. That means being able to process files, organize their contents, and retrieve relevant material in response to a question.

The project is still under development. The broader research and reasoning experience described on this page represents its intended direction, not a claim that every capability is already complete.

01

Document processing

Extracting text from documents so their contents can be searched.

02

Semantic retrieval

Finding related passages based on meaning, not only exact words.

03

Local model integration

Connecting retrieved document content to a locally run model.

FUTURE DIRECTION

More than finding a single passage.

The longer-term direction is to make Archivist useful for more involved research tasks, where an answer may depend on information found in several different documents.

01 / CONNECT

Understand relationships

Bring together relevant information from different files and help users see how separate details relate to one another.

02 / INVESTIGATE

Explore complex questions

Support questions that require comparing information, identifying patterns, or combining evidence from several sources.

03 / REVIEW

Inspect the results

Make it easier to review the material behind an answer and decide whether a conclusion is actually justified.
